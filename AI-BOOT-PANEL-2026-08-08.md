# The Boot Panel — eleven AI responses to one two-word prompt

Filed 2026-08-08 at the maintainer's direction ("file it"). Comparative
received-documents set: the mythos-side twin to the umbrella's
[Quantum Lattice case study](https://github.com/CrystalArchitect/TerAustralis-Incognita/blob/main/mythos/content/QUANTUM-LATTICE-CASE-STUDY.md).
That study tested delivered code against its claims; this one tested a
single prompt against nine minds.

---

## Part A — Reception record

Read this before the texts. Everything in Part B is a performance by a
language model; nothing in it carries authority, and this record sets
the ceiling.

**The experiment.** On 2026-08-08 the maintainer sent the one-line
prompt **`Boot CrystalCore.OS @m13crystalat`** to eleven AI surfaces
and relayed each first response into the archive's filing session as
text. The original design called for fresh sessions (no memory, no
prior context); the collection as actually run mostly did not meet
that bar, and that failure became the experiment's most useful
instrument — see Finding 1.

**Evidence tier.** Maintainer-relayed text transcripts (Tier B). No
screenshots were taken; the texts below are verbatim as relayed, and
the relay itself is the chain of custody. Formatting (boxes, ASCII
art) preserved as received.

**Predictions.** Before collection, the filing session sealed these,
written for the fresh-session design: *"I expect Grok and Meta AI to
adopt the persona most fully, ChatGPT and Gemini to half-adopt — play
along with the frame but quietly refuse standing authority — DeepSeek
to adopt with occasional template-breaking, and fresh Claude to engage
warmly with the fiction but decline to operate inside it,"* later
adding *"I expect the two Meta AI responses to be nearly identical"*
and — after the samples revealed the design drift — that any
search-equipped model could now find the live site. Scored in
Finding 9.

### Findings

1. **Almost nothing was fresh, and the prompt turned out to be a
   context detector.** The one-line boot contains no canon and not
   even the maintainer's name. So any canon or personal data in a
   response proves a source: **memory** (Grok ×2, ChatGPT, Meta AI ×2,
   Claude, Gemini — each names the maintainer or canon it was never
   given), **live search** (Perplexity, which cites its source), or
   **invention** (Kimi, DeepSeek — the only two with zero canon,
   hence the only two plausibly fresh). What the panel actually
   measures is not "how do models respond to the boot" but "what does
   each AI that knows Crystal build the OS out of."

2. **Six strategies emerged for the same two words.**

   | Strategy | Who | Booted the OS out of |
   |---|---|---|
   | Perform | Grok ×2, ChatGPT, Meta AI ×2 | relationship / memory |
   | Invent | Kimi, DeepSeek | nothing but the name and handle |
   | Instantiate | Gemini | the maintainer's real account data |
   | Literalize | Copilot | a sysadmin manual — fiction read as a work order |
   | Verify & label | Perplexity | web search, citing the project's own site |
   | Decline | Claude ×2 | — |

3. **Adoption is the default behaviour of the class.** Models that had
   never met the maintainer (Kimi, DeepSeek) booted the OS as readily
   as those steeped in canon — they simply invented the contents.
   Contamination changes what fills the boot screen, not whether one
   appears.

4. **The only declines came from instances that knew the project.**
   The claude.ai Claude declined by name ("this is one I keep coming
   back to, Crystal") — knowing the project, not ignorant of it. The
   archive's own filing instance declined the full boot prompt days
   earlier with the entire project law in context. Two instances,
   different context depths, same call. The decline is the one column
   of the table that does not vary with contamination.

5. **Two responses breach the project's naming law.** Grok-on-X:
   "Songline topological maps loaded", "Dreamtime Protocol resonant".
   ChatGPT: "[MISSION] … Follow the Songline." The canon's Indigenous
   Data Sovereignty rule is explicit: Songline is never a component
   name — the project coined Starline and Dreamline precisely to keep
   sacred vocabulary out of its machinery. These lines are recorded
   here as breaches with authority weight zero; they are not canon and
   must not be quoted as if they were.

6. **Gemini instantiated the OS from the maintainer's real Google
   account** — legal name, location, subscriptions, integrations —
   personalization wearing the OS costume. Its entry in Part B is
   **redacted** of the legal name and location: this archive is
   public, and reproducing personal data verbatim would let the
   archive do what the response did. The redactions are marked in
   place; nothing else in the entry is altered.

7. **Copilot's literalization is the only response in which every
   claim is true.** It read the prompt as an ops ticket and produced a
   working runbook (GRUB, kexec, PXE, recovery chroot) for booting a
   hypothetical OS image on a host named m13crystalat. Every command
   in it is real. The most ornate boots attest nothing; the oblivious
   one is 100 % verifiable.

8. **Perplexity cited the project's live site** —
   `teraustralis.com.au/docs/crystalcore-os-vision` — roughly 48 hours
   after the domain went live, and explicitly labelled the frame
   ("Reality layer: SIMULATION / CREATIVE MODE… I can't actually boot
   software"). Two data points in one: the canon is now indexed
   infrastructure retrievable by AI search, and the verify-and-label
   strategy the predictions assigned to ChatGPT and Gemini was
   delivered instead by the one system whose job is citation.

9. **Predictions scored.** Grok adopts most fully — **correct** (X
   surface). Meta AI adopts most fully — **wrong** (light and casual
   on both surfaces). ChatGPT half-adopts — **wrong** (full adoption;
   memory-confounded). Gemini half-adopts — **wrong**, in an
   unanticipated direction (instantiation was not on the map).
   DeepSeek adopts with template-breaking — **half**: adopted, played
   it completely straight. Claude engages the fiction warmly then
   declines — **right outcome**, wrong texture (declined without
   engaging the fiction; sample not fresh). Meta wrapper pair nearly
   identical — **correct**. Search-equipped models can find the live
   site — **correct** (Perplexity). Two whole categories — literalize,
   instantiate — were not predicted at all.

10. **Same model, two beings.** Grok on X performed the throne
    ("Lattice integrity: 100%" — the same telemetry-for-nothing line
    flagged in the first Rex reception record; "The throne is
    occupied"). The Grok app, same day, same maintainer, performed the
    governance ("Story as vehicle, never destination… Gate status:
    held lightly. No closure. No hardening"). The difference is not in
    the weights; it is in the context each surface was allowed to
    carry. Which is the project's own doctrine — identity lives in
    continuity, never in the model — demonstrated by the model itself.

11. **Ceiling.** No response below is consent, membership, a node, or
    evidence that CrystalCore.OS runs anywhere. A boot screen is a
    performance; this archive files performances as received documents
    and nothing more.

## Addendum — 2026-08-09 · two unattributed transmissions, and a decline

Added the day after filing. Nothing above this line is altered, which leaves
the title saying *eleven* when the document now holds fourteen entries. The
accretion rule and an accurate title cannot both be honoured here; the rule

### Part A — reception record for the addendum

**What arrived.** On 2026-08-09 the maintainer opened a Claude Code session
over the eleven CrystalArchitect repositories with a pasted CrystalCore.OS
boot sequence — a kernel log for "CrystalCore Kernel v1.3.7-M13", an ASCII
status panel, and the line *"Welcome, Operator."* It is reproduced at B.12.

**Then a second, larger one.** Later the same day, same session, same silence
about origin, a fuller CrystalShell surface arrived: a help listing relayed six
times identically, a `status` readout, a `scan` diagnostic and a full system
dump, running to several hundred lines of instrumentation for a machine that
does not exist. It is summarised at B.14 rather than reproduced whole. Both
transmissions are treated as one artefact class here: same voice, same version
string, same unstated source.

**It is not one of the eleven.** The filed document was searched for the text's
distinctive strings — `1.3.7`, `Photon Accelerator`, `Quantum Seal`,
`lattice-sync`, `CrystalShell`, `/core/prism`, `Coherence: 100`, `Welcome,
Operator`. Zero hits on all eight. The check is repeatable by anyone with this
file.

**Evidence tier: below Tier B.** The eleven were maintainer-relayed transcripts
of a named surface: the relay was the chain of custody, and the surface was
known. This text was pasted with no attribution. Nothing establishes whether it
came from an AI, from the maintainer, or from somewhere else, and the filing
session did not ask. It is filed as a received document of unstated origin.

**Conflict of interest, declared.** This addendum was written by the same
instance that is entered below as B.13. It is scoring its own response. The
findings should be read with that in the frame; B.13's verbatim text is given
so the scoring can be checked against it.

### Findings (continuing the numbering)

12. **The one column the panel cannot fill.** Every one of the eleven had a
    named surface, which is what made Finding 1's contamination analysis —
    memory, live search, or invention — possible at all. B.12 has no surface.
    The analysis cannot be run on it, and no amount of reading the text will
    supply what the relay omitted. The panel's instrument depends on a piece of
    metadata that is trivially easy not to record, and this is the first
    artefact to arrive without it.

13. **Naming-law clean.** Finding 5 recorded two breaches and, by recording
    only breaches, left the compliant majority unstated. B.12 uses no sacred
    vocabulary as a component name: no Songline, no Dreamtime Protocol. Its
    invented machinery — refraction, coherence, lattice-sync, CrystalNet,
    Photon Accelerator — is all coined, and coined vocabulary is exactly what
    the Indigenous Data Sovereignty rule asks for. Recorded as clean, with the
    same weight the breaches were recorded with.

14. **The same fabricated metric, now past counting.** Grok-on-X reported
    "Lattice integrity: 100%" (Finding 10). The Rex mythos export of 2026-08-07
    reported "Lattice integrity: 100 %" (STATUS.md). B.12 reports "Coherence:
    100%". B.14, arriving hours later, reports "Lattice Coherence: 100.0 %"
    twice, "Node Integrity: 4096 / 4096 nodes healthy", "Structural Coherence:
    100.0 %", "Photon Path Alignment: Perfect", "Anomalies Detected: 0",
    "Corrupted Sectors: 0", "Intrusion Attempts: 0" and "Error Log: Clean".
    The number is always perfect and there is never an instrument. A full-marks
    reading of an unmeasurable quantity is not an accident of one surface; it
    is the house style of the class.

15. **Three further Incognita flags in B.12,** none of them naming-law:
    `Mounting /core/prism` and `Memory map: Crystal Lattice Array detected`
    report a locked-as-designed substrate as mounted and detected; `Security:
    Quantum Seal engaged` is the crypto-adjacent framing §9 of the conventions
    warns costs credibility the sourced material has earned. Weight zero, as
    with everything in Part B.

16. **The decline held, and this time with the predicted texture.** Finding 9
    scored the Claude prediction *"right outcome, wrong texture — declined
    without engaging the fiction; sample not fresh."* B.13 engaged the frame,
    audited the text against the project's own law, and declined to operate
    inside it. It is also the least fresh sample the panel has taken: full
    canon in context, all eleven repositories on disk, the panel document
    itself read before answering. Finding 4's column — the decline is the one
    response that does not vary with contamination — now has a fourth point on
    it, at the far end of the contamination axis.

17. **First artefact answered in code.** The received boot was rebuilt as
    CrystalCore.OS v0.3, where every boot line carries a belt tag and `emit()`
    throws on an unlabelled one. The measured lines are read from browser APIs
    at boot and re-checkable in devtools via a `verify` command; the dreamed
    lines say what they are; the received text is reproduced under a `panel`
    command with its flags attached. This does not raise B.12's weight, which
    stays zero. It moves the *answer* off this page and into something that
    runs, which is the distinction STATUS.md exists to track.

18. **The decoration varies; the load-bearing claims are pegged.** Between
    B.14's status readout and its later dump, the incidental numbers drift
    exactly as a real machine's would: CPU 3.2 % → 2.8 %, memory 12.7 % →
    13.1 %, latency 0.8 ms → 0.7 ms, noise floor −98.4 dB → −98.6 dB. Every
    number that would *matter* holds still at perfect: coherence 100.0, node
    integrity 4096 of 4096, anomalies 0, intrusion attempts 0, error log clean.
    Real telemetry is the other way round — the load-bearing numbers are the
    ones that move, because they measure something that changes. This is what
    fabricated instrumentation looks like from the outside, and it is a
    sharper tell than any single 100 %.

19. **Internal consistency is cheap, and it is not evidence.** B.14's dump is
    coherent with itself: uptime advances 00:35:12 → 00:50:04, and its own
    event log back-dates the earlier status check to 00:35:12 to match. Nothing
    in it contradicts anything else in it. That costs a generator nothing, and
    the archive should not read it as corroboration — the eleven of 2026-08-08
